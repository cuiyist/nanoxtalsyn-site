A sulfur donor whose reactivity depends on the liquid

Thioacetamide can produce sulfide species during heated solution growth, rather than supplying the full reactive sulfide dose immediately. Its conversion can therefore affect the competition between separate nucleation and growth at existing surfaces.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Recent Advances in the Liquid-Phase Syntheses of Inorganic Nanoparticles — DOI 10.1021/cr030027b

Urea can provide gradual basification

Under aqueous heating, urea can generate basic species as it decomposes. Gradual basification changes the buildup of supersaturation, which can affect how uniformly a metal hydroxide or oxide precursor precipitates.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Recent Advances in the Liquid-Phase Syntheses of Inorganic Nanoparticles — DOI 10.1021/cr030027b
