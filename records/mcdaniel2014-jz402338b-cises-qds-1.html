Chemical intuitionReferenced context and interpretation

Mechanistic interpretations and related literature remain separate from the experimental training labels.

No separately reviewed chemical-intuition essay is attached to this record yet.

Octadecene is not always inert

Octadecene provides a weakly coordinating medium for hot solution growth, leaving the ligands more freedom to control surface binding. It can also polymerize under growth conditions, leaving an alkene-derived residue that is difficult to separate from hydrophobic nanocrystals.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: The Trouble with ODE: Polymerization during Nanocrystal Synthesis — DOI 10.1021/acs.nanolett.9b03088

Selenium delivery from TOP–Se

Trioctylphosphine selenide carries selenium in a soluble feed, rather than as separate solid selenium. The conversion of that feed can affect how much selenium is available while nuclei form and grow.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Synthesis and characterization of nearly monodisperse CdE (E = sulfur, selenium, tellurium) semiconductor nanocrystallites — DOI 10.1021/ja00072a025

A thiol can both supply sulfur and bind the surface

In metal-sulfide routes that use an alkanethiol as the sulfur feed, metal–thiolate chemistry can supply sulfur to the growing lattice. Thiol or thiolate species can also bind at the particle surface, so sulfur delivery and surface capping may be linked.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Selective Nanocrystal Synthesis and Calculated Electronic Structure of All Four Phases of Copper–Antimony–Sulfide — DOI 10.1021/cm5005642

Indium carboxylate as a precursor

Indium acetate can be exchanged into a more soluble indium carboxylate by a long-chain acid before a phosphorus reagent is added. Conversion of the carboxylate and the formation of intermediate clusters can separate precursor consumption from the emergence of larger crystals.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Two-Step Nucleation and Growth of InP Quantum Dots via Magic-Sized Cluster Intermediates — DOI 10.1021/acs.chemmater.5b00286
