05

Chemical intuitionReferenced context and interpretation

Mechanistic interpretations and related literature remain separate from the experimental training labels.

No separately reviewed chemical-intuition essay is attached to this record yet.

Cadmium oxide needs conversion to a soluble feed

Cadmium oxide is a solid precursor that can be converted into a soluble cadmium complex by an acidic ligand. That conversion makes it possible to control the cadmium delivered during nucleation without using a volatile organocadmium feed.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Formation of High-Quality CdTe, CdSe, and CdS Nanocrystals Using CdO as Precursor — DOI 10.1021/ja003633m

A phosphine can carry a chalcogen feed

Trioctylphosphine can solubilize elemental selenium or tellurium as a phosphine chalcogenide for colloidal growth. Delivering the chalcogen in this bonded form changes the chemistry that supplies reactive growth species.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Synthesis and characterization of nearly monodisperse CdE (E = sulfur, selenium, tellurium) semiconductor nanocrystallites — DOI 10.1021/ja00072a025

A reactive sulfur feed

Bis(trimethylsilyl)sulfide can supply sulfur rapidly to a reactive metal precursor in a nonaqueous colloidal route. The resulting rate of sulfur delivery affects the period over which new nuclei can form.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Colloidal PbS Nanocrystals with Size‐Tunable Near‐Infrared Emission: Observation of Post‐Synthesis Self‐Narrowing of the Particle Size Distribution — DOI 10.1002/adma.200305395

TOPO as a coordinating medium

Trioctylphosphine oxide supplies a coordinating medium in which metal chalcogenide nanocrystals can nucleate and grow. Coordination at the particle surface influences which growth sites remain accessible and how the particles disperse afterward.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Synthesis and characterization of nearly monodisperse CdE (E = sulfur, selenium, tellurium) semiconductor nanocrystallites — DOI 10.1021/ja00072a025
