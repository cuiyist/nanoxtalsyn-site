Chemical intuitionReferenced context and interpretation

Mechanistic interpretations and related literature remain separate from the experimental training labels.

No separately reviewed chemical-intuition essay is attached to this record yet.

Preparing a selenium feed in phosphine

Elemental selenium powder can be dissolved in trioctylphosphine to prepare a soluble feed for cadmium selenide hot injection. Preparing the stock first separates selenium dissolution from the injection that starts nanocrystal nucleation.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Synthesis and characterization of nearly monodisperse CdE (E = sulfur, selenium, tellurium) semiconductor nanocrystallites — DOI 10.1021/ja00072a025

Cadmium oxide needs conversion to a soluble feed

Cadmium oxide is a solid precursor that can be converted into a soluble cadmium complex by an acidic ligand. That conversion makes it possible to control the cadmium delivered during nucleation without using a volatile organocadmium feed.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Formation of High-Quality CdTe, CdSe, and CdS Nanocrystals Using CdO as Precursor — DOI 10.1021/ja003633m

A phosphonate can bias shape evolution

Long-chain phosphonic acids can modify both cadmium availability and surface chemistry in wurtzite cadmium selenide growth. Changes in those processes can favor axial growth over growth across the rod.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Mechanisms of the Shape Evolution of CdSe Nanocrystals — DOI 10.1021/ja0027766

Nucleation and size focusing

A rapid change in precursor availability can create a short nucleation period followed by growth on existing particles. Growth can narrow the size distribution while material is supplied, whereas later ripening can consume smaller particles and change that distribution again.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Mechanisms of Nucleation and Growth of Nanoparticles in Solution — DOI 10.1021/cr400544s
