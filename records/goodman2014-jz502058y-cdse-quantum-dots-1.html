Chemical intuitionReferenced context and interpretation

Octadecene is not always inert

Octadecene provides a weakly coordinating medium for hot solution growth, leaving the ligands more freedom to control surface binding. It can also polymerize under growth conditions, leaving an alkene-derived residue that is difficult to separate from hydrophobic nanocrystals.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: The Trouble with ODE: Polymerization during Nanocrystal Synthesis — DOI 10.1021/acs.nanolett.9b03088

Cadmium oxide needs conversion to a soluble feed

Cadmium oxide is a solid precursor that can be converted into a soluble cadmium complex by an acidic ligand. That conversion makes it possible to control the cadmium delivered during nucleation without using a volatile organocadmium feed.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Formation of High-Quality CdTe, CdSe, and CdS Nanocrystals Using CdO as Precursor — DOI 10.1021/ja003633m

Technical-grade TOPO is not always chemically equivalent to purified TOPO

Phosphorus-containing impurities in technical-grade TOPO can change cadmium precursor coordination and surface binding during colloidal growth. They can therefore change shape selection without a change in the nominal solvent name.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: The Trouble with TOPO; Identification of Adventitious Impurities Beneficial to the Growth of Cadmium Selenide Quantum Dots, Rods, and Wires — DOI 10.1021/nl801692g

A long-chain primary amine coordinates metal sites

During amine-assisted colloidal growth, octadecylamine can coordinate surface metal sites while keeping the surface compatible with a nonpolar liquid. Changing the capping layer changes which sites remain available for growth.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Oleylamine in Nanoparticle Synthesis — DOI 10.1021/cm4000476
