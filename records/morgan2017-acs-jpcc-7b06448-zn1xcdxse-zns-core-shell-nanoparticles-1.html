Changing cations in an existing solid

Cation exchange changes the cation population of an existing material while transport and interface motion govern the transformation. Retention of an anion framework or particle shape depends on the particular system.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Forging Colloidal Nanostructures via Cation Exchange Reactions — DOI 10.1021/acs.chemrev.5b00739

Carboxylic-acid and carboxylate ligands

Carboxylic acids and carboxylates can associate with metal-containing surfaces and solution species through their oxygen groups. The hydrocarbon part of a long-chain ligand can favour dispersion in a compatible solvent, while acid-base equilibria influence how its head group associates.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Prospects of Colloidal Nanocrystals for Electronic and Optoelectronic Applications — DOI 10.1021/cr900137k

From a metal precursor to reactive species

A metal precursor supplies the metal, while its ligands and conversion chemistry influence how reactive species become available. Stronger or more persistent coordination can slow release of reactive metal species, while ligand exchange can open alternative conversion pathways.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Mechanisms of Nucleation and Growth of Nanoparticles in Solution — DOI 10.1021/cr400544s
