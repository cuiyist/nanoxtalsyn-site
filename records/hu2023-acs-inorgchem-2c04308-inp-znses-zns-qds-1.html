Chemical intuitionReferenced context and interpretation

Mechanistic interpretations and related literature remain separate from the experimental training labels.

No separately reviewed chemical-intuition essay is attached to this record yet.

A phosphine can carry a chalcogen feed

Trioctylphosphine can solubilize elemental selenium or tellurium as a phosphine chalcogenide for colloidal growth. Delivering the chalcogen in this bonded form changes the chemistry that supplies reactive growth species.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Synthesis and characterization of nearly monodisperse CdE (E = sulfur, selenium, tellurium) semiconductor nanocrystallites — DOI 10.1021/ja00072a025

Aminophosphine supplies phosphorus through a different pathway

In aminophosphine-based indium phosphide synthesis, ligand exchange and phosphorus conversion can occur before the particles grow. This changes the relation between precursor consumption, nucleation, and the supply of phosphorus to existing particles.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Aminophosphines: A Double Role in the Synthesis of Colloidal Indium Phosphide Quantum Dots — DOI 10.1021/jacs.6b01254

A primary amine can change surface growth

Oleylamine can coordinate soluble metal species and bind exposed surface sites during colloidal growth. It can therefore change how rapidly precursors react and how readily the resulting particles remain dispersed.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Oleylamine in Nanoparticle Synthesis — DOI 10.1021/cm4000476

Zinc halide changes the coordination environment

A zinc halide additive can participate in the coordination chemistry of aminophosphine-based indium phosphide growth without being the phosphorus source. That environment can change the growth and surface chemistry of the resulting nanocrystals.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Aminophosphines: A Double Role in the Synthesis of Colloidal Indium Phosphide Quantum Dots — DOI 10.1021/jacs.6b01254
