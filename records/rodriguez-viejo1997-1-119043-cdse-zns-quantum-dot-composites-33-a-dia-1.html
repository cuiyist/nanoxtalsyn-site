Precursor delivery and surface chemistry

Vapour-deposition methods separate precursor delivery from reactions at or near a surface. Adsorbed precursors can react, exchange ligands or desorb, with surface coverage and delivery affecting which processes dominate.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Atomic Layer Deposition: An Overview — DOI 10.1021/cr900056b

Phosphines and precursor solutions

Elemental selenium can react with organophosphines to form soluble phosphorus-selenium-containing precursor species. Organic substituents can make these species compatible with an organic medium, while their composition and reactivity depend on the phosphine and preparation conditions.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Recent Advances in the Liquid-Phase Syntheses of Inorganic Nanoparticles — DOI 10.1021/cr030027b

Phosphine-oxide coordination

A phosphine oxide can interact with suitable precursor or surface sites through its oxygen donor. Competition among oxygen-donor ligands can change precursor coordination and the availability of surface sites.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Recent Advances in the Liquid-Phase Syntheses of Inorganic Nanoparticles — DOI 10.1021/cr030027b
