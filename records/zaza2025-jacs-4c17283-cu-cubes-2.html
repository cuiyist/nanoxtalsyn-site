A primary amine can change surface growth

Oleylamine can coordinate soluble metal species and bind exposed surface sites during colloidal growth. It can therefore change how rapidly precursors react and how readily the resulting particles remain dispersed.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Oleylamine in Nanoparticle Synthesis — DOI 10.1021/cm4000476

TOPO as a coordinating medium

Trioctylphosphine oxide supplies a coordinating medium in which metal chalcogenide nanocrystals can nucleate and grow. Coordination at the particle surface influences which growth sites remain accessible and how the particles disperse afterward.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Synthesis and characterization of nearly monodisperse CdE (E = sulfur, selenium, tellurium) semiconductor nanocrystallites — DOI 10.1021/ja00072a025

Stable cluster intermediates can delay particle growth

In some indium phosphide routes, unusually stable molecular clusters accumulate instead of turning immediately into a smoothly growing distribution. Their appearance breaks the simple link between mixing the precursor and forming the final nanocrystals.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Two-Step Nucleation and Growth of InP Quantum Dots via Magic-Sized Cluster Intermediates — DOI 10.1021/acs.chemmater.5b00286
