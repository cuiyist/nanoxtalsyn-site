Octadecene is not always inert

Octadecene provides a weakly coordinating medium for hot solution growth, leaving the ligands more freedom to control surface binding. It can also polymerize under growth conditions, leaving an alkene-derived residue that is difficult to separate from hydrophobic nanocrystals.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: The Trouble with ODE: Polymerization during Nanocrystal Synthesis — DOI 10.1021/acs.nanolett.9b03088

A solvated tellurium feed for chalcogenide growth

Trioctylphosphine telluride allows tellurium to be supplied in a soluble phosphine-bound form. When tellurium conversion competes with other chalcogen sources, their different delivery rates can influence which material forms at each growth stage.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Fabrication of CuInTe2 and CuInTe2–xSex Ternary Gradient Quantum Dots and Their Application to Solar Cells — DOI 10.1021/nn401274e

Indium carboxylate as a precursor

Indium acetate can be exchanged into a more soluble indium carboxylate by a long-chain acid before a phosphorus reagent is added. Conversion of the carboxylate and the formation of intermediate clusters can separate precursor consumption from the emergence of larger crystals.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Two-Step Nucleation and Growth of InP Quantum Dots via Magic-Sized Cluster Intermediates — DOI 10.1021/acs.chemmater.5b00286

Nucleation and size focusing

A rapid change in precursor availability can create a short nucleation period followed by growth on existing particles. Growth can narrow the size distribution while material is supplied, whereas later ripening can consume smaller particles and change that distribution again.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Mechanisms of Nucleation and Growth of Nanoparticles in Solution — DOI 10.1021/cr400544s
