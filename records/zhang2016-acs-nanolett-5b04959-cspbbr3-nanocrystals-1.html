05

Chemical intuitionReferenced context and interpretation

Mechanistic interpretations and related literature remain separate from the experimental training labels.

No separately reviewed chemical-intuition essay is attached to this record yet.

Oleic acid helps put cesium into a soluble feed

Oleic acid can convert cesium carbonate into cesium oleate for hot solution synthesis of cesium lead halide nanocrystals. Preparing that feed separately helps control when cesium becomes available to form the crystalline phase.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Nanocrystals of Cesium Lead Halide Perovskites (CsPbX 3 , X = Cl, Br, and I): Novel Optoelectronic Materials Showing Bright Emission with Wide Color Gamut — DOI 10.1021/nl5048779

Octadecene is not always inert

Octadecene provides a weakly coordinating medium for hot solution growth, leaving the ligands more freedom to control surface binding. It can also polymerize under growth conditions, leaving an alkene-derived residue that is difficult to separate from hydrophobic nanocrystals.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: The Trouble with ODE: Polymerization during Nanocrystal Synthesis — DOI 10.1021/acs.nanolett.9b03088

Oleate and the nanocrystal surface

Bound oleate can stabilize dispersed metal chalcogenide nanocrystals, and not all surface carboxylate need occupy the same binding environment. Displacing metal carboxylate from the surface can change surface stoichiometry as well as colloidal stability.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Ligand Exchange and the Stoichiometry of Metal Chalcogenide Nanocrystals: Spectroscopic Observation of Facile Metal-Carboxylate Displacement and Binding — DOI 10.1021/ja4086758

A primary amine can change surface growth

Oleylamine can coordinate soluble metal species and bind exposed surface sites during colloidal growth. It can therefore change how rapidly precursors react and how readily the resulting particles remain dispersed.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Oleylamine in Nanoparticle Synthesis — DOI 10.1021/cm4000476
