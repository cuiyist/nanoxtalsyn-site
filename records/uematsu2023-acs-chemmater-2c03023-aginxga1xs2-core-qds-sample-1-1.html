Chemical intuitionReferenced context and interpretation

Tributylphosphine as a chalcogen carrier

Tributylphosphine can react with elemental chalcogen to form a soluble molecular phosphine chalcogenide feed. When that feed supplies growing nanocrystals, its conversion rate affects the availability of chalcogen for nucleation and growth.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Molecular Control of the Nanoscale: Effect of Phosphine–Chalcogenide Reactivity on CdS–CdSe Nanocrystal Composition and Morphology — DOI 10.1021/nn301182h

A primary amine can change surface growth

Oleylamine can coordinate soluble metal species and bind exposed surface sites during colloidal growth. It can therefore change how rapidly precursors react and how readily the resulting particles remain dispersed.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Oleylamine in Nanoparticle Synthesis — DOI 10.1021/cm4000476

A nonpolar liquid disperses hydrocarbon-capped particles

Hexane can redisperse nanocrystals whose surface ligands present hydrocarbon groups to the liquid. Its compatibility with the capping layer contrasts with the poor-solvent conditions used to precipitate that same dispersion.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Synthesis and characterization of nearly monodisperse CdE (E = sulfur, selenium, tellurium) semiconductor nanocrystallites — DOI 10.1021/ja00072a025

Antisolvent washing

When acetone serves as an antisolvent for hydrophobically capped particles, it can move the crystals into a precipitate while some free ligand remains in solution. Repeated precipitation and redispersion can change the amount of loosely associated material left at the surface.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Prospects of Colloidal Nanocrystals for Electronic and Optoelectronic Applications — DOI 10.1021/cr900137k
