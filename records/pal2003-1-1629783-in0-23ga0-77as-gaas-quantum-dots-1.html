Arsenic supply sets the growth surface environment

The group-V to group-III supply ratio helps determine how arsenic-rich the surface remains during indium arsenide growth. Changing arsenic pressure at a fixed indium supply can alter surface incorporation and the way strained islands grow.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Arsenic pressure and spacer layer thickness effects on the optical properties of stacked InAs/InAlAs quantum dot array — DOI 10.1002/pssc.200460465

Strain and growth interruptions affect epitaxial islands

In strain-driven indium arsenide epitaxy, a wetting layer can give way to islands as the growing film relieves lattice strain. During a growth interruption the surface can continue to reorganize, so the island population need not remain fixed when the incoming flux changes.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Reflection high-energy electron diffraction observation of the dynamics of semiconductor quantum dot formation and decay — DOI 10.1116/1.1515912
