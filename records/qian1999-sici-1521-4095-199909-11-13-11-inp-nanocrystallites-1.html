Solvent and a closed reaction environment

A heated closed solvent environment changes solubility, transport and precursor conversion compared with an open vessel. Changes in solvent properties and dissolution followed by reprecipitation can alter the pathways available for nucleation and crystal growth.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Hydrothermal technology for nanotechnology — DOI 10.1016/j.pcrysgrow.2007.04.001

Conversion of a nonmetal precursor

Chalcogen and pnictogen precursors can differ in how they release or transfer the element during a reaction. Bonding, coordination and the reaction medium can influence the activation or transfer steps that make an element available for growth.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Prospects of Colloidal Nanocrystals for Electronic and Optoelectronic Applications — DOI 10.1021/cr900137k

Solvent and transport

A solvent carries dissolved species and transports heat through a reaction or dispersion. Solubility, viscosity and molecular interactions affect how precursors and ligand-coated particles move and mix.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Recent Advances in the Liquid-Phase Syntheses of Inorganic Nanoparticles — DOI 10.1021/cr030027b
