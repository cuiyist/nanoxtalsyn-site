Octadecene is not always inert

Octadecene provides a weakly coordinating medium for hot solution growth, leaving the ligands more freedom to control surface binding. It can also polymerize under growth conditions, leaving an alkene-derived residue that is difficult to separate from hydrophobic nanocrystals.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: The Trouble with ODE: Polymerization during Nanocrystal Synthesis — DOI 10.1021/acs.nanolett.9b03088

A phosphine can carry a chalcogen feed

Trioctylphosphine can solubilize elemental selenium or tellurium as a phosphine chalcogenide for colloidal growth. Delivering the chalcogen in this bonded form changes the chemistry that supplies reactive growth species.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Synthesis and characterization of nearly monodisperse CdE (E = sulfur, selenium, tellurium) semiconductor nanocrystallites — DOI 10.1021/ja00072a025

A zinc carboxylate carries zinc into a nonpolar phase

Zinc stearate provides a zinc species compatible with a hydrophobic growth medium. Conversion of that feed and its binding environment can influence whether zinc reaches existing surfaces or contributes to other growth.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Prospects of Colloidal Nanocrystals for Electronic and Optoelectronic Applications — DOI 10.1021/cr900137k

A reactive phosphide feed changes growth timing

Tris(trimethylsilyl)phosphine reacts with indium carboxylate in common colloidal indium phosphide routes. Rapid precursor conversion does not necessarily make all particles form at once, because molecular clusters can participate before continued crystal growth.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Two-Step Nucleation and Growth of InP Quantum Dots via Magic-Sized Cluster Intermediates — DOI 10.1021/acs.chemmater.5b00286
