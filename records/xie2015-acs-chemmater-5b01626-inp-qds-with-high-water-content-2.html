Transport in a flowing reaction

Flow geometry changes how reactants mix and how long material remains in different parts of a device. Diffusion and interfacial transport can therefore matter even when the overall feed composition is unchanged.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Microfluidics: Fluid physics at the nanoliter scale — DOI 10.1103/RevModPhys.77.977

Phosphines and precursor solutions

Elemental selenium can react with organophosphines to form soluble phosphorus-selenium-containing precursor species. Organic substituents can make these species compatible with an organic medium, while their composition and reactivity depend on the phosphine and preparation conditions.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Recent Advances in the Liquid-Phase Syntheses of Inorganic Nanoparticles — DOI 10.1021/cr030027b

From a metal precursor to reactive species

A metal precursor supplies the metal, while its ligands and conversion chemistry influence how reactive species become available. Stronger or more persistent coordination can slow release of reactive metal species, while ligand exchange can open alternative conversion pathways.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Mechanisms of Nucleation and Growth of Nanoparticles in Solution — DOI 10.1021/cr400544s
