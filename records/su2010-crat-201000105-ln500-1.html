Contact and transport in solids

Solid-state conversion depends on contact between reactants and transport across their interfaces. Mechanical activation can create fresh interfaces and defects, which can help subsequent chemical conversion.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Mechanochemistry: opportunities for new and cleaner synthesis — DOI 10.1039/C1CS15171A

From a metal precursor to reactive species

A metal precursor supplies the metal, while its ligands and conversion chemistry influence how reactive species become available. Stronger or more persistent coordination can slow release of reactive metal species, while ligand exchange can open alternative conversion pathways.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Mechanisms of Nucleation and Growth of Nanoparticles in Solution — DOI 10.1021/cr400544s

Conversion of a nonmetal precursor

Chalcogen and pnictogen precursors can differ in how they release or transfer the element during a reaction. Bonding, coordination and the reaction medium can influence the activation or transfer steps that make an element available for growth.

General background, not the authors' interpretationReference: Prospects of Colloidal Nanocrystals for Electronic and Optoelectronic Applications — DOI 10.1021/cr900137k
